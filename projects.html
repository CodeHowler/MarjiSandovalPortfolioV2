Operations & Product Design In Development

Waypoint

A trucking operations platform concept designed around the information drivers need while managing loads, customer locations, trip details, and daily operations.

Workflow Design · Requirements · Logistics · Product Planning

Explore the project

The Mission

Simplify the work that happens when the truck is parked.

Waypoint is an evolving trucking operations platform concept inspired by my firsthand experience managing the administrative and financial side of commercial trucking.

The Problem

Commercial drivers manage much more than driving. Load assignments, appointments, customer information, trip notes, settlement records, and financial calculations are often spread across disconnected systems, spreadsheets, and paperwork.

Organizing that information takes valuable time away from drivers' limited off-duty hours.

From TruckerPay to Waypoint

TruckerPay began as a software development bootcamp capstone focused on driver compensation and load information. The original idea grew out of years spent coordinating loads, tracking expenses, reviewing settlements, and maintaining records.

Waypoint represents an expanded product vision: bringing the operational and financial information drivers need into one organized platform.

Product Vision & Requirements

I defined target user groups, identified recurring operational problems, organized proposed functional modules, and established principles to guide product decisions.

These principles include minimizing duplicate data entry, reducing manual calculations, supporting mobile workflows, and keeping frequently needed information easy to access.

Business Process Analysis

I documented three core operational workflows:

  1. Load Review and Feasibility: Evaluating whether an assignment can reasonably be completed based on available information, timing, safety, equipment, hours-of-service considerations, and financial factors when applicable.
  2. Trip Planning: Organizing accepted loads into operational trips, including pickup and delivery, fuel, rest, and other necessary stops.
  3. Trip Execution and Ongoing Planning: Addressing schedule changes, additional assignments, delays, reassignment, and differences between planned and actual activity.

Key Design Decisions

An important distinction in the process documentation is that a Load represents a freight assignment, while a Trip represents the driver's operational journey. A single Trip may contain multiple Loads, and some Stops do not belong to a particular Load.

The proposed design also preserves historical information when circumstances change. For example, a reassigned Load should retain records of the original driver's activity instead of erasing completed work.

Scope & Roadmap

The product vision outlines a phased approach beginning with core load, customer, dashboard, pay, and reporting capabilities.

Future phases explore broader business management, document handling, integrations, analytics, and intelligent assistance.

Waypoint is intended to complement existing electronic logging devices and carrier systems, not replace them.

Current Development Status

Waypoint is currently in product definition, requirements analysis, and design. Existing documentation includes the product vision, business processes, proposed architecture, business rules, and technical planning materials at varying levels of completion.

Detailed user stories have not yet been developed. The original TruckerPay application provides an earlier software development foundation, but Waypoint's broader planned capabilities should not be considered implemented features.

Software Development Work in Progress

TruckerPay

A C#/.NET web application project exploring ways to organize driver compensation, weekly pay, shipment records, and related operational details.

C# · .NET MVC · Data Modeling · Financial Workflows

Explore the project

The Original Problem

Commercial drivers often need to independently track loads, verify mileage, and estimate weekly earnings. Compensation can involve different rates for loaded and empty miles, along with bonuses, advances, and deductions.

TruckerPay began as my software development bootcamp capstone, inspired by firsthand experience managing these records manually.

The Solution

I developed a C# ASP.NET MVC web application to organize load records and support weekly driver pay calculations.

The project combines record-management functionality with compensation calculations based on load information and pay periods.

Application Architecture

I organized the solution into four projects, separating the MVC web application, data models, data access, and service logic.

The application uses Entity Framework for database operations and dedicated models for creating, editing, listing, and displaying records.

Load Management

The application includes controller and service methods for creating, retrieving, updating, and deleting load records.

Load information includes shipper and receiver names, pickup and delivery appointments, and related operational notes.

Weekly Pay Calculations

I implemented service methods that calculate loaded miles, empty miles, total mileage, and estimated pay using load-pay records associated with a pay period.

The calculation logic also incorporates compensation adjustments and deductions, reflecting the financial details drivers may need to review when checking settlements.

User-Specific Data

The service layer uses user identifiers to scope database queries. For example, load retrieval checks both the requested record ID and its associated owner ID.

This was an important consideration when designing an application intended to manage individual drivers' records.

From TruckerPay to Waypoint

Building TruckerPay helped clarify that driver compensation was only one part of a much larger operational challenge.

That experience contributed to the development of Waypoint, a broader product concept focused on trip planning, customer information, financial visibility, and reducing administrative work for drivers.

Project Status

TruckerPay is an existing bootcamp-era application that has been opened and run successfully in Visual Studio 2026. It remains a development project rather than a publicly released product.

Technologies & Skills

C# • ASP.NET MVC • Entity Framework • LINQ • CRUD Operations • Layered Application Design • Business Logic • Data Modeling

Research & Writing Ongoing

Family History Book

A long-form family history project combining archival research, genealogy, historical context, and narrative writing across multiple generations.

Research · Source Verification · Writing · Information Design

Explore the project

The Project

What began as a family history research project has grown into an illustrated historical narrative tracing generations of ancestors, their communities, and the journeys that shaped our family.

The book is being developed as a personal gift, combining genealogical research with accessible storytelling.

The Research Challenge

Historical records are often incomplete, contradictory, difficult to read, or scattered across multiple archives and publications.

Establishing reliable family connections requires comparing sources, reconciling conflicting information, and separating documented facts from assumptions.

My Research Approach

I work backward through generations using genealogical records, historical publications, vital records, probate documents, and other available evidence.

I examine discrepancies in names, dates, locations, and relationships before incorporating findings into the family narrative.

Historical Context & Migration

The research explores family connections across New England, Atlantic Canada, and earlier ancestral locations.

Geographic research and migration timelines help explain how families moved between communities and how those movements influenced later generations.

Evidence Evaluation

When records conflict or relationships cannot be established confidently, I identify the uncertainty rather than presenting an unsupported conclusion as fact.

Research notes, source comparisons, and outstanding questions help guide further investigation.

Turning Research Into Stories

The challenge extends beyond identifying names and dates. I organize the research into chapters that connect individual lives with their historical settings.

The goal is to create a readable family history that preserves the underlying research while bringing earlier generations to life.

Current Status

Research and manuscript development are ongoing. Several ancestral lines and individual chapters have been drafted, while other relationships require additional documentation and verification.

The completed book is intended for family use. Selected research examples may be shared separately as portfolio samples.
